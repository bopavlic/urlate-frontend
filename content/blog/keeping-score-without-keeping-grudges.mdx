“You keep track of who’s late? That sounds petty.”

It’s a fair first reaction. Writing down your friends’ failings doesn’t sound like the foundation of a healthy relationship. But in practice, the people who count out loud tend to be less annoyed than the people who don’t. Here’s why.

## Counting in silence is the dangerous kind

If someone keeps you waiting often enough, you’re already counting. Everyone does. The difference is whether it’s written down where both of you can see it.

Minutes counted in silence only ever add up. You remember every time they were late and forget the time you were. It never gets settled, because there’s nothing to settle. Eventually it leaks out as a sigh, a sharp comment, or a fight about something else entirely.

Counting together is different. It’s accurate, it counts both of you, and it can be settled. That alone takes most of the sting out.

## Numbers are funnier than complaints

“You’re always late” is a complaint. “You’re 18 minutes in the red” is a bit. Turning lateness into a number moves it from a character flaw to something you can tease each other about.

That’s the tone [Ur Late](/) is built around: a friendly tease, always on the side of the person who waited, never mean. It’s a running joke between people who like each other, not a punishment tool.

## Settling up is part of the game

A grudge has no end. Minutes owed do. With Ur Late, the person who’s owed can forgive some minutes or reset everything to zero, whenever they like. Some people do it on birthdays. Some trade it for a coffee. Some never reset and just enjoy watching the number climb.

What matters is that settling is possible and it’s a choice. Nothing ever resets by itself, and nobody can quietly rewrite the history.

## When to stop counting

Counting minutes works when it’s mutual and light. If one person hates it, or the number starts feeling like a weapon, stop. The point was never the minutes. It was having a way to talk about them that makes you both laugh.

## So, is it petty?

A little. That’s the fun of it. But it’s a lot less petty than silently counting, and a lot more honest. Write the minutes down, count both ways, and let the person who waited decide when to call it square.
