Everyone has one. The friend who says “five minutes away” from the shower. The sibling you tell to be ready at six so they’re ready at quarter past. You like them. You just also like being on time.

Most of us handle it in one of three ways, and none of them work very well.

## The three usual moves

**Nagging.** You mention it. Then you mention it again. It lands as criticism, they get defensive, and next week nothing has changed except the mood in the car.

**Fuming quietly.** You say nothing and keep a tally in your head instead. The tally only ever goes up, it never gets shared, and one day it comes out all at once over something small.

**Gaming the clock.** You tell them the film starts at 7:30 when it starts at 8. It works until they figure it out, and then you’re both lying about times.

## What actually helps

Lateness is rarely about not caring. People run late because they underestimate how long things take, because they hate waiting more than they hate being late, or because nobody has ever shown them the number. That last one is the useful bit.

**Make it visible, not personal.** “You’re always late” is an accusation. “You were six minutes late today” is a fact. Facts are easier to laugh about.

**Count both ways.** If you’re going to keep score, keep it honestly. The first time you’re the late one and log it yourself, the whole thing stops being an attack and starts being a game.

**Let it be funny.** The friends who stay friends through years of lateness are usually the ones who turned it into a running joke. A joke needs a punchline, and “you owe me 43 minutes” is a pretty good one.

**Agree on stakes, or don’t.** Some people settle up: every 30 minutes owed is a coffee. Some just enjoy watching the number grow. Both are fine. Decide together.

## Where Ur Late fits

This is the whole reason [Ur Late](/) exists. It started with a brother who was a minute or two late to football practice almost every time. Add it all up, and the minutes tell a story neither of you could argue with.

You log who was late and by how much. Ur Late nets out what you’ve waited for each other into one number, and the other person sees it too. No nagging, no secret tally, no fake start times. Just the score.

If your friend never improves, at least you’ll know exactly how many minutes of your life they owe you. And if they do improve, you’ll have the receipts to prove it.
